# Agentic AI governance operating model

Illustrative design for a synthetic enterprise. Not an account of the portfolio owner's professional accomplishments.

## The mandate record

Before an agent receives a tool credential, record: business purpose, accountable owner, users/tenants, allowed tools and resources, data classification ceiling, deployment environments, destinations, cost limits, approval roles, renewal date, monitoring owner and intervention procedure.

## Ownership

| Decision / operation | Accountable owner | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Define business purpose and acceptable impact | Business/service owner | Approved mandate and measurable success criteria |
| Classify data and authorize use | Data owner | Inventory labels and use constraints |
| Approve permissions and connector boundaries | Security owner | Threat model, scoped grants and connector review |
| Accept residual risk / production exceptions | Risk owner | Time-bound acceptance and independent approval |
| Enforce mediation and operate the gate | Platform owner | Configuration, deployment checks and availability evidence |
| Monitor control failures | Security operations owner | Alerts, escalation thresholds and response runbooks |
| Stop, contain and recover | Incident commander | Stop receipt, connector revocation, recovery decision |
| Renew or retire the agent | Business owner with security review | Performance, exceptions, drift and expiry review |

Approval is meaningful only when these roles map to authenticated individuals with delegated authority. The library's strings are synthetic identifiers.

## Rollout gates

1. **Sandbox:** use synthetic data and fixture tools. Demonstrate denial, approval, expiry, replay and incident scenarios.
2. **Bounded pilot:** authenticate identities, isolate credentials, enforce connector mediation and externally retain evidence. Choose a narrow reversible workflow.
3. **Production readiness:** exercise timeouts and recovery after an ambiguous action; verify budget metering and resource labels; obtain explicit residual risk acceptance.
4. **Ongoing assurance:** review permission drift, exception age, stop effectiveness, unresolved execution states and actual business outcomes. Reapprove material changes.

## Executive measures

Recommended measures to design, not claimed results: percentage of agent actions mediated; unapproved impactful attempts; exceptions past expiry; resource-label completeness; time to stop new reservations; unresolved ambiguous executions; independent audit checkpoint coverage; owner-approved mandate renewal rate.

Do not present synthetic demo counts as production performance. A low denial rate alone is not evidence of a secure agent—it may indicate weak policy or insufficient testing.

## Incident decision sequence

Stop new reservations, revoke or isolate connector credentials, identify already-reserved actions, preserve evidence with an independent checkpoint, assess affected resources, authorize recovery explicitly, then decide whether to revise or retire the mandate. Releasing a stop is an accountable decision, not an automated retry.

## Board-level questions

- Which business processes can an agent materially affect?
- Can it expand its own authority or reach data beyond its purpose?
- Who approves a production action, and can the proposer approve itself?
- What happens if an action completes but its receipt does not?
- What evidence would let an independent reviewer reconstruct the decision?
- When should a mandate expire, be reapproved or be revoked?
